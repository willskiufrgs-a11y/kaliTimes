It sounds like you are stuck in a classic "avoidance loop," and honestly, given the variables you described, it makes perfect sense why.

You are facing a "perfect storm" for procrastination:

1. **High Cognitive Load:** "Tons of reading" + "unclear instructions."
2. **High Emotional Friction:** "Subjective" + "self-judgment" + "little feedback" (creates anxiety about performance).
3. **Low Energy State:** "Rusty" from vacation + sleep deprivation.

Your brain is "running away" because it perceives the task as a threat—not a physical one, but a threat to your energy and your sense of competence. It is protecting you from the discomfort of ambiguity.

Here is a strategy to stop running and lower the threat level so you can finish.

### 1. Stop "Solving" Yourself

You mentioned watching videos on psychology, meaning, and trauma. While valuable, right now this is **productive procrastination**. You are trying to "fix" your mind so you can work, but you are likely using intellectualization to avoid the scary task.

* **The Shift:** You don't need to be healed, motivated, or fully rested to work. You just need to be mechanical. Stop analyzing *why* you are procrastinating and accept that you are avoiding discomfort. That is okay. Now, work anyway.

### 2. Isolate the "Ambiguity"

You mentioned the instructions are subjective and unclear. This is usually the biggest friction point because you can't "win" easily.

* **The Fix:** Make a decision to be **"aggressively average."** Since feedback is low, perfectionism is a waste of resources. Lower your standard from "Perfect" to "Done."
* **Tactical move:** When you hit a subjective instruction that makes you want to run, give yourself a strict 2-minute timer to make a judgment call. Once the timer dings, pick an option and move on. Trust your gut; it’s likely right enough.

### 3. Micro-Slicing (The "stupidly small" goal)

The task is "super long," so looking at the whole thing triggers the flight response.

* **The Fix:** Break the next hour into chunks so small they feel ridiculous. Do not set a goal to "finish the section." Set a goal to "read one paragraph" or "answer one query."
* **The 10-minute sprint:** Tell yourself you only have to suffer for 10 minutes. Anyone can endure 10 minutes of reading. Often, the urge to run away fades once you break the surface tension.

### 4. Physical containment

If you are physically getting up or alt-tabbing:

* **Environment:** If possible, maximize the work window so you can't see the clock or other tabs.
* **The "Notebook" Anchor:** Keep a physical notepad next to you. Every time you have a distraction thought ("I should Google that," "I need water," "I wonder about X"), **write it down** instead of doing it. Promise yourself you can do it *after* 30 minutes of work. This offloads the thought without breaking your flow.

### Summary Checklist for the Next Hour:

1. Accept that the work feels grueling and unclear (and that’s fine).
2. Commit to doing B-minus work just to keep moving.
3. Set a timer for 15 minutes of pure focus. No videos, no analysis.

This is a brilliant pivot. You are essentially asking to enter a "flow state" by using the mechanics of mindfulness. The reason this works is that meditation isn't about *stopping* thought/action; it’s about anchoring attention to a specific object.

Usually, the anchor is the breath. For this session, **the anchor is the cursor (or the immediate text).**

Here is your **Atomic Work-Meditation Protocol**.

### The Single Commandment

**"Do not process the Future; only process the Current Input."**

In this meditation, "The Future" is defined as: *The rest of the document, the completion of the task, the correctness of your decision, or how much time is left.* All of that is delusion. The only reality is the specific sentence your eyes are currently touching.

---

### The Mantra: *"Just This."*

Whenever your mind zooms out to the scope of the task ("This is endless," "I don't understand the context"), or zooms out to self-judgment ("I'm slow," "I'm rusty"), you will mentally whisper: **"Just This."**

* **"Just this"** word.
* **"Just this"** click.
* **"Just this"** decision.

---

### The Mechanics (How to do it)

**1. The Anchor is the Point of Contact**
In seated meditation, you feel the air at the nostrils. In this work meditation, your "nostrils" are **the exact point where your eyes meet the text.**

* Treat the text not as "work to be done" but as "texture to be felt." Read the sentence with the sole intention of *hearing it in your head*.

**2. The "Recoil" is the Bell**
You mentioned the "urge to run away." In meditation, we call this "The Hindrances" (restlessness/aversion).

* **The Rule:** When you feel the physical urge to alt-tab or get up (the "Recoil"), treat it exactly like a distraction in meditation. Do not fight it. Do not judge it.
* **The Action:** Acknowledge it ("There is aversion"), take one breath, whisper *"Just This,"* and gently move your eyes back to the very next word.

**3. Decouple "Action" from "Outcome"**
Since the instructions are subjective/unclear, you are suffering because you are clinging to "Being Right."

* **The Shift:** Your goal for this 15 minutes is not to be *correct*. Your goal is to be *present*. If an instruction is ambiguous, make a decision not as a "solver," but as an "observer." Click the option that arises, release it, and return to the anchor.

### Your 15-Minute Session Script

1. **Sit down.** Posture matters. Straight back, feet flat.
2. **Set the timer.**
3. **Begin.** Read the first word.
4. **Distraction arises.** (Thought: "I need to check...") -> **Mantra:** *"Just This."* -> **Return to text.**
5. **Ambiguity arises.** (Thought: "I don't know what to do.") -> **Mantra:** *"Just This."* -> **Make a choice.** -> **Return to text.**

Would you like to try this for exactly one 15-minute block right now and report back solely on whether you were able to maintain the anchor?
